# Game 2 computation log

## Finite-mixture expected payout

`games/four_way/best_response.py` enumerates the `C(K+2,3)` unordered opponent triples for a support of size `K`, applies their iid multiplicities, and computes a candidate payout against that supplied mixture. `best_candidate` only compares a caller-supplied action set; it is not a full-game oracle. `response_distribution.py` audits a fixed strategy with exact rational arithmetic.

A diagnostic population assigns equal probability to the even allocation `(20,20,20,20,20)` and five single-castle corners. Against it, the exhaustive full-action search finds `(1,21,21,21,36)` with payout `8.402778`, a gain of about `4.652778` above the symmetric self-play value 3.75. Adding that response at equal mass yields another profitable response `(2,1,22,22,53)` with payout `8.291545`. This confirms the simple baseline is highly exploitable, not that the expansion process converges. Results are in `results/full_action_responses.json`.

Against four copies of the even allocation, `(20,0,21,21,38)` earns 9, illustrating a profitable response to a pure strategy.

## Further search and structural result

Restricted replicator plus full-action oracle experiments are documented in `restricted-search.md`. On 9-, 10-, and 11-action pools, the restricted dynamics produced mixtures against which full-action search printed `9.0` at floating-point precision. Exact rational audits show why that is not a guarantee of 9 against every support triple. A pure symmetric allocation cannot be a Nash equilibrium; see the direct deviation proof in `restricted-search.md`.

A native C++ restricted multiplicative-weights implementation agrees with the Python reference on the six-action pool to within `5.6e-16`. On 24 actions, restricted payoff gaps fell below `1e-14` while a full-action reply still earned 8.8482; the response-expanded pool remained exploitable. See [`double-oracle.md`](double-oracle.md) and `results/double_oracle_chain.json`.

The exact integer oracle precomputes each opponent triple's per-castle score increments and parallelizes the legal-allocation scan. With eight threads it exactly searched the 18-action equal-weight pool (1,140 unordered triples), finding `(15,24,28,32,1)` with payout `8.6314300412` and exact exploitability `4.8814300412` in about eight seconds. The denominator `1,000,008` preserves the exact uniform `1/18` weights, so the reported interval is a point. See `results/exact_response_diverse18.json`.

A larger 25-action replicator time-average mixture had eight nonzero actions after largest-remainder quantization to `N=1,000,002`. The exact oracle found `(24,30,35,0,11)` with payout `8.999999999785`. Exact total-variation accounting between the decimal input and its rationalized mixture gives a certified original-mixture exploitability interval of `[5.2499919565, 5.25]`; see `results/exact_response_pool25.json`. This confirms the near-9 deviation was not just long-double rounding. Both results are fixed-mixture audits, not population solutions.

## Restricted symmetric minimax and response expansion

For a symmetric population `p`, let `g_a(p)` be a pure action's expected rank payout against three iid draws from `p`. The constant-sum identity gives `sum_a p_a g_a(p)=15/4`, so `max_{a in P} g_a(p) >= 15/4` for any pool `P` containing the support. A restricted symmetric Nash equilibrium attains equality. `symmetric_minimax_expansion.py` minimizes this cubic max on each finite pool with multistart SLSQP, then sends candidate mixtures to the exact full-action oracle and adds top responses. Its restricted payoff tensor is generated by the tested native kernel in `restricted_payoff_tensor.cpp`. The nonlinear solver is only a local candidate generator; only the exact response bounds are global certificates.

Starting from the 25-action replicator pool plus its exact full-game reply, a single-response expansion reached an exact-audited best gap of `2.5847541` after 25 expansions (candidate support 17; response `(25,10,3,62,0)`, payout `6.3347541`). The multistart/top-K continuation subsequently improved the best audited gap: the integer-frequency candidate (`N=10^9`, support 35) has exact exploitability `1.6999488442` (response `(7,4,10,74,5)`, payout `5.4499488442`; pool size 110). The conservative total-variation-adjusted upper bound for the optimizer's decimal weights is `1.6999488813`. Restricted equilibria need not improve monotonically—the next response can exploit the mixture again. This is progress, not an equilibrium. HPG job `49375316` continues from the pool-82 checkpoint with a six-hour limit. Earlier result: `results/symmetric_minimax_expansion/hpg-round-025.json`; the current job writes its checkpoints under `results/hpg_symmetric_minimax_multistart/`. The exact oracle supports `--top-k K` to return the K best allocations with exact payout ordering.

## Restricted support search (sizes one through four)

`restricted_two_support_search.py` enumerates pure and two-action supports in a finite pool. For a pair `(a,b)`, the indifference condition `g_a(x)=g_b(x)` is cubic in `x=P(a)`; unit-interval roots are isolated numerically, rationalized to integer frequencies, and audited with the exact global response oracle.

`restricted_support3_search.py` builds the small-pool payoff tensor, uses multi-start Newton to find roots of the two indifference equations on each three-action support, checks every omitted pool action, and exact-audits surviving candidates against the whole game. On the six-action baseline it finds a restricted support-three equilibrium, but the exact full-game response `(20,21,21,0,38)` gives exploitability `21/4 = 5.25`. On the diverse 18-action pool, a denominator-8 support-three multistart grid found 181 numerical roots but no restricted equilibrium; this does not prove none exists. A denominator-8 support-four Newton search on the six-action baseline found no roots, also without proving nonexistence. Support-two searches on the six-action and 18-action pools find the same minimum gap. These results illustrate why restricted equilibria do not certify full-game equilibria.

```sh

  research/four_way/baseline_population.json \

PYTHONPATH=src python3 research/four_way/restricted_two_support_search.py \
  research/four_way/results/diverse-pool18.json \
  --max-actions 18 --output research/four_way/results/two_support_diverse18.json
PYTHONPATH=src python3 research/four_way/restricted_support3_search.py \

```

These are numerical candidate generators, not complete algebraic support solvers; they do not search supports larger than four. Root tolerance and frequency rounding preclude an equilibrium claim, while the reported full-action deviation gap is exact for each rationalized mixture.

## Current status / what remains

No Game 2 equilibrium has been certified. Game 2's payoff depends on the final ranking against three opponents, so castle marginals do not make utility separable as in Game 1. The response-expanded restricted minimax search has lowered its best exact-audited gap to about `1.6999`, but this remains large and positive; restricted stability is not full-game stability. The exact response runner also supports `--max-support K`: it solves against the retained highest-mass actions, computes exact total variation from the supplied rational weights (including quantization/pruning), and reports a two-sided exploitability interval. This helps only when discarded mass is small and the retained-mixture response is low; discarding half the equal six-action baseline's mass yields only the trivial 9-point upper bound. A zero global deviation gap is still needed for an equilibrium claim. Monte Carlo evaluation remains diagnostic only.

Tests cover multiset aggregation, the constant-sum invariant, support-two cubic roots, support-three and support-four derivatives, exact response ordering (including top-K), and the exact response kernel. They do not certify a Game 2 equilibrium.
